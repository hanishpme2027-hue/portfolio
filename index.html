Selected projects

Three designs I modeled in SolidWorks.

Power Generating Speed Bump

An assembly model of a speed bump designed to generate power. The sectioned view shows a curved top plate over five racks that drive pinion gears, with springs at the ends.

  • Curved top plate that takes the vehicle load
  • Five vertical racks meshing with pinion gears
  • Cylindrical units mounted on the pinion shafts
  • Springs at the sides, in a transparent housing shown in section
Where it is used
Places where vehicles slow down anyway, such as road approaches, parking entrances, toll plazas and gated campuses.
How it works
A vehicle presses the top plate down. The racks move with it and turn the pinion gears, which drive the units on the shafts. The springs push the plate back up for the next vehicle.
Expected outcome
Part of the energy a slowing vehicle would otherwise lose is turned into electricity. This can feed low-power loads such as a streetlight or sign. Output rises with vehicle weight, speed and traffic.

Lever Arm

A lever arm modeled in SolidWorks. A cylindrical hub with a central bore sits at one end and connects to an angled arm that ends in a grip.

  • Cylindrical hub with a through bore
  • Three through-holes of different sizes in the arm
  • Grooved grip end for better hold
  • Filleted edges and a stepped, angled arm
Where it is used
Levers like this operate machine controls, linkages, clamps and brakes. The hub mounts on a shaft and the grip end is where the hand applies force.
Expected outcome
Force applied at the grip is carried along the arm to the hub, and the extra length gives mechanical advantage. The holes allow pins or linkages to be attached.
